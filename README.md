**Catch 151** (until 2.2.0 called PokeKnomiGochi) is a Pokémon game for the **BTT Knomi V2**, the little round screen made for 3D printers, and for your Mac, your Windows PC and your browser. Raise any of the original 151 as it walks through its own pixel world, battle and catch wild Pokémon, climb Knomi Tower, fill your [Pokédex](https://fedorsosnin.github.io/Catch151/sprites/), and play seven mini games by tapping and tilting, the newest Pinball on a round table and a fishing contest off the Beach's pier, with a live [minigame leaderboard](#minigame-leaderboard) for each one's best.

**[Listen to the soundtrack](https://fedorsosnin.github.io/Catch151/music/)**: 31 original chiptune songs, written for the game and played live in your browser by its own synthesizer, with a scope for each of the four voices. See [Original music and sound](#original-music-and-sound).

**[Catch 151 Dex](https://fedorsosnin.github.io/Catch151/sprites/)**: all 151, regular and shiny, in every view the game has (standing, turning, walking, from behind, asleep, attacking and more), sorted by number or type, each credited to its artists. Each one's page says what the game's Pokédex says about it, with its stats and every move it learns; pick a few to see their match-ups, and tick the ones you have. Its **Catching** tab gives the chances of each star, 1★ to 3★, regular and shiny: of a catch, and of a Search in its zone or a Wild Encounter turning it up that way.

**[Minigame Leaderboard](#minigame-leaderboard)**: each minigame's best, live from copies of the game everywhere, with the gamer tag that set it and the day.

## What's in it

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **A world that walks with you.** Your Pokémon walks toward you while its home slides by: grass and flowers stream past, the hills sway, clouds drift and birds fly over. Five hand-drawn zones, the Field, Forest, Beach, Cave and Mansion, and outdoors the light goes from day to dusk to night.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Choose your partner.** Bulbasaur, Charmander, Squirtle and Pikachu walk up to meet you. Later, choose any first form you've caught, shinies included.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Battle wild Pokémon.** Turn-based fights with each Pokémon's own moves from Red and Blue, learned as it levels up, in the scene of the zone you're hunting.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Catch them.** Throw a Poké Ball at the Pokémon running back and forth: it hits, drops to the ground and shakes, one, two, three...

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Climb Knomi Tower.** Walk the pier out to the tower's islet and take your Pokémon and two friends from your Pokédex up eight floors of type guardians, with HP carrying over from fight to fight. At the top waits the Legend, and then Mewtwo: beat them both for a Master Ball, every time you do.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Ultra Balls and Master Balls.** Every battle you win fills two bars, which pop up after each fight to show how close you are: an Ultra Ball every 25, which triples your catch chance, and a Master Ball every 100, which always catches.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **The Repel.** Ten minutes of mini games earn a Repel. Spray it from the backpack and weaker wild Pokémon stay away for 3 minutes, so the ones you're hunting turn up.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Fill the Pokédex.** All 151, with their shiny forms, each caught with 1 to 3 stars. Every entry turns round to show its Pokémon from all sides. **[View the Pokédex](https://fedorsosnin.github.io/Catch151/sprites/)**: all of them, regular and shiny, in every view.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Travel Knomi Island.** Five zones on a round island that's alive: foam runs round the coast, the pines sway and the Mansion's windows light up and go dark. See what's still missing in each, then follow the pier out to Knomi Tower.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Play pinball.** New: **Pinball**, on a round table that is the screen itself. Shoot the ramp, run the orbit to light the berries and hit the three bumpers for Diglett, who evolves into Dugtrio. Roll the ball into the tall grass (at night, a cave) and a wild Pokémon that lives there jumps into the ring in the middle: every hit of the ball is your Pokémon's move.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Go fishing.** New: **Reel Fishing**, five casts off the Beach's pier. Pick a fish by its shadow, strike when the float goes under and reel it in to its own beat, then weigh it and let it go. The score is the total weight, against bronze, silver and gold.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Seven mini games.** Berry Drop, Rock Hopper, PikaSurf, Dungeon Crawler, Who's That Pokémon?, Reel Fishing and Pinball, on cartridges in a Game Boy, four a page. **Play Again!** at the end.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Buddies.** Knomis, Macs, PCs and browsers on the same Wi-Fi visit each other. Their Pokémon walk beside yours, give it a Party Boost, and battle, trade and play games for two.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **A trophy room.** All 27 medals as things in one room: badges in a case (Knomi Tower's too), statues, cups, a golden cartridge for holding a high score, a shiny gem, a gleam sweeping over the ones you've won. Tap a group for its shelf, then a medal for how to earn it.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Care for it.** Feed it berries from the backpack, give it a bath and turn the lights off for bed. It evolves when it's ready, and one day it says goodbye and walks off over the hill.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Music and cries.** On the Mac, Windows and the web, original chiptune plays on every screen: a home tune that turns slow and dreamy at night, a Travel tune and a battle theme for each zone, Knomi Tower, the legends, the mini games and more, 31 tracks in all. Every Pokémon has its own cry, when you pet it and when it wins. Sound (the effects and cries) and Music each have their own level from 0 to 3 in Settings. (The Knomi has no speaker, so it stays quiet.) **[Listen to the soundtrack](https://fedorsosnin.github.io/Catch151/music/)**, and see [Original music and sound](#original-music-and-sound).

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Hold it for a menu.** Hold your finger on your Pokémon for a ring of Settings, Medals, Stats and the Pokédex.

<img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pokeball.png" width="18" alt=""> **Everywhere, in 8 languages.** The Knomi, Mac, Windows and the web, in English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese and Korean. Updates install themselves, and your Pokémon is kept.

## New: Pinball

<p align="center"><img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/pinball.gif" alt="Pinball on the meadow table: the ball rolls into the tall grass with a !, a wild Butterfree jumps out into the ring in the middle, Diglett pops up between the bumpers and is hit, Squirtle's Bite lands on Butterfree with each hit of the ball, a shot goes up the ramp, and Butterfree is knocked out: YOU WON! +500" width="360"></p>

The seventh cartridge, on the Game Boy's second page: pinball on a round table, the round screen itself, its rim the wall. Hold the left or right half of the screen to raise that flipper (the Knomi's button raises both; on a computer the left and right arrows, and Space for both). Shoot the ramp up the left into the top corner, arrows chasing up it, and the ball comes back down a wire rail to your left flipper (+250). Send it round the orbit under the wire to light the six berries: light them all and every point after counts double (the next six, triple). Hit all three bumpers, a Poké Ball, a Great Ball and an Ultra Ball, and Diglett pops out of its hole between them: hit it before it ducks back down (+1000). Beat it twice and the next one evolves into Dugtrio, three heads to hit (+1000 each, +2000 more for all three). Roll the ball into the tall grass by the right wall, where something is always rustling, and a wild forest or field Pokémon of about your level jumps out into the ring in the middle of the table. Every hit of the ball on it is your Pokémon's own move, with its real damage and type matchups (+50), its HP over it and 20 seconds on the gold ring to knock it out (+500). Three balls, and the best score is kept.

Each game deals one of two tables at random, a night-blue one on a chrome rim, where the grass is a cave and cave Pokémon come out of it, or a meadow on a wooden one, and the ball is a Poké Ball that rolls as it goes. On a computer and the web it has a song of its own, [▶&nbsp;Pinball](https://fedorsosnin.github.io/Catch151/music/#pinball), a little faster while a wild one is in the ring.

## New: Reel Fishing

<p align="center"><img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/fishing.gif" alt="One cast of Reel Fishing: the float flies out, dips at two nibbles and goes under with a !, the Tentacool is reeled in to its beat with the LINE bar's white needle on the right, leaps out and is weighed on the gold gauge on the left: 42.3 kg" width="360"></p>

The sixth cartridge on the Game Boy, and a fishing contest: five casts off the end of the pier at the Beach, the rod in your hands. The water holds the Beach's Pokémon as shadows, small ones near the pier and heavy ones out in the deep, and the bigger the shadow, the heavier the fish. Tap the water by the one you want, wait out the nibbles and strike when the float goes under. Then reel it in by tapping to its beat: each weight has its own pace, quick for the small ones and slow and steady for the heavy ones, and the **LINE** bar on the right shows the line's strain, its white needle rising with every tap and sinking as the fish pulls. Keep it in the green: too fast and the line snaps, too slow and the fish slips off. Every fish is weighed, a gold gauge on the left filling to its weight and saying how big it is for one of its kind, then it goes back in the water. Five casts add up to your score, against bronze, silver and gold lines at 110, 331 and 661 lb, and one giant swims in every game, Gyarados or Lapras, the hardest fish of all to land.

It all shows on the screen, so it plays as well on the Knomi, which has no speaker (its backlight flashes at the bite). On a computer and the web it has its own sounds and two new songs, the fight song playing at the fish's pace, and phones and controllers buzz along.

## The Knomi Tower Battle System

<p align="center"><img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/tower-climb.gif" alt="A climb of Knomi Tower: the tower's islet, the tower, the team, who goes first, a floor fight, both balls' bars, the Rock Badge, the Legend, Mewtwo, Tower Master and the challenges" width="360"></p>

Walk out along Knomi Island's new pier to **Knomi Tower**: eight floors, each guarded by one boss of its type, and at the top the Legend, and after it **Mewtwo**. You climb with your Pokémon and two friends from your Pokédex, at your Pokémon's level and training, and their HP carries over from fight to fight, so who you send in matters as much as how strong they are. Before every fight, **Who goes first?** marks who should win it, worked out from the fight itself. Every fight fills your Ultra and Master Ball bars, a lost climb ends with a tip for the next one and your friends cheer you on, and beating the Legend earns a **Master Ball, every time**. The first team to do it goes into the **Hall of Fame**, and then there are three challenges to beat it again.

## How to play

The screen turns to follow however you hold the Knomi. It starts ready for your right hand with the cable going off to the left, so the button is under your finger.

See [How it works](Catch151/docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md) for charts of what every action, game, item and zone does, and [Moves](Catch151/docs/MOVES.md) for when every Pokémon learns each move, and how strong each one is. [Evolution stats](Catch151/docs/EVOLUTION.md) lists every evolution and the HP, ATK, DEF and SPD it adds.

| Do this | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Tap the Pokémon** | Pet it |
| **Hold your finger on it** | The menu: a ring opens round it. Slide onto one and lift, or let go and tap one. Keep holding still for 3 seconds to let your Pokémon go (or bring back its goodbye, if you chose to stay together). When it's ready to evolve, the same hold offers **Evolve** too |
| **Tap the icons along the bottom** | From left to right: play mini games, Travel to hunt, open the backpack in the middle (berries, candy and your Repels), turn the light off for bed, or give it a bath |
| **Stats** in the ring | Its card: profile, battle stats, training, progress, and a page for each item |
| **Tap its name on the card** (✏️) | Rename it |
| **Pokédex** in the ring | Tap a Pokémon for its page, where it turns round, and tap it there to give it a pat |
| **Medals** in the ring | The trophy room: every medal. Tap anywhere to see how to earn each one |
| **Settings** in the ring | The clock and your time zone (or set the time, without Wi-Fi), sound and language; the next page for Wi-Fi and updates; then **About**: the version, and the GitHub and YouTube links as QR codes |
| **The arrows at the sides**, or a swipe | Turn the pages. "Page 2 of 7" shows where you are |
| **Tap the round ✕ at the bottom** | Close a menu, the card, the Pokédex or the clock |
| **Press and slide** | The button under your finger shows large above it. Slide to another one if you missed, and lift to choose |
| **Press the button** | Start a wild battle |
| **Hold the button for 1–3 seconds** | Turn the screen on or off |
| **Hold the button for 10 seconds** | Asks whether to erase the game and start over |

While your Pokémon sleeps, the home screen dims; menus you open over it stay bright.

### Evolution Stats

Evolving makes your Pokémon stronger. Its stats come from its species' base HP, ATK, DEF and SPD, which are bigger for each evolved form, and it keeps its level and all its training, so it gets the bigger numbers the moment it evolves. Charmander's line, for example:

| | Evolves at | HP | ATK | DEF | SPD |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Charmander | 16 | 39 | 52 | 43 | 65 |
| Charmeleon | 36 | 58 (+19) | 64 (+12) | 58 (+15) | 80 (+15) |
| Charizard | | 78 (+20) | 84 (+20) | 78 (+20) | 100 (+20) |

**[Every Pokémon's evolutions and stats](Catch151/docs/EVOLUTION.md)** has all 151, line by line, with what each evolution adds.

### Your first Pokémon, and the next

The first time, Bulbasaur, Charmander, Squirtle and Pikachu walk toward you through the Field. Tap one to stop them and choose it.

When your Pokémon is ready to evolve, a red **Evolve** button appears under it, with a grey **Not now**: tap **Evolve** to evolve, or **Not now** to keep its form. It won't ask again; whenever you change your mind, hold your finger on it for 3 seconds and tap **Evolve**. [Moves](Catch151/docs/MOVES.md) shows the level each species evolves at.

A fully evolved Pokémon that is at least 3 days old offers a goodbye. If you choose to stay together, it won't ask again: whenever you're ready, hold your finger on it for 3 seconds to bring the goodbye back. You can also let any Pokémon go the same way. Either way, it turns, walks off up the hill and over it, and you choose your next one, who walks in from the distance.

Until a Pokémon has been offered its goodbye, the next one is one of the four starters again. After that, you choose from your Pokédex: any first form you've caught, so Pikachu, not Raichu, with that form's stars. A shiny you have is its own square with sparkles, and picking it gives you that shiny. If you neglect a Pokémon until it runs away, you choose the same way.

Levels stop at **100**, since a Pokémon past that would be too strong to battle; one that is already older stays at 100. For the end game, raising 3, 5 and 10 different Pokémon to level 100 earns the **Level 100 ×3**, **×5** and **×10** medals.

### Battles and catching

Press the button, or tap **Wild Encounter** in Travel, to meet a wild Pokémon in a simple turn-based fight, inspired by the Game Boy games: your Pokémon stands with its back to you, facing the wild one. Every Pokémon fights with its own moves from Pokémon Red and Blue and learns stronger ones as it levels up, and each move shows a little of its type: flames, splashing water, leaves, sparks, ice and more.

Winning trains your Pokémon. Losing earns nothing and leaves it nearly out of energy, so it has to sleep before it can fight again. **RUN** leaves a wild battle, but 1 time in 20 it fails: the button turns red and says **Couldn't get away!**, and you have to fight it out. A battle with a buddy can always be left.

Every wild Pokémon you beat can be caught, even one you already have. It runs back and forth across the field trying to get away, at its own speed: a Slowpoke ambles, an Electrode darts, and the quick ones sometimes turn back halfway. Your balls sit along the bottom: tap one to choose it, then the **Throw** button above them, in the ball's colour and with its name, throws it; with only Poké Balls, the button is ready and a tap on the ball throws too. Lean the Knomi left and right to steer (on a computer, the left and right arrows choose a ball, Space or the up arrow throws it, and the arrows steer it). Rings show around the Pokémon as it runs: land the ball in the small green circle and it always holds, in the next ring three times in four, and in the outer ring one time in four. A ball that hits bounces off, drops to the ground and shakes, one, two, three, then the Pokémon joins your Pokédex or breaks free and runs away. You get three Poké Balls a catch.

**Stars:** every Pokémon in your Pokédex has 1 to 3 ★, its genes kept simple, and the regular and shiny forms each have their own. Once the ball holds, a screen of its own shows the catch and its stars. A new one goes into your Pokédex; if you already have it and the new one has more stars, it replaces yours. A 3★ form gets three small gold stars under it in the Pokédex grid, and choosing a 3★ form as your next Pokémon gives it better stats.

About 1 wild Pokémon in 100 is **shiny**: official shiny colors with gold sparkles around it. The Pokédex keeps shiny and regular forms apart and shows both side by side once you have both, and each can be traded on its own.

### Items

Three items you earn by playing, kept with you from one Pokémon to the next (up to 99 of each). Each has its own page on the card, with how far along the next one is.

| Item | How to earn it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Ultra Ball** | Every 25 battles you win | Triples your catch chance when it hits. |
| **Master Ball** | Every 100 battles you win | Always catches the Pokémon it hits. |
| **Repel** | Every 10 minutes of mini games | Spray it from the backpack: for 3 minutes, weaker wild Pokémon stay away. |

Running away, battles with buddies and Dungeon Crawler don't count toward the balls. Ultra and Master Balls sit beside your Poké Balls on the catch screen while you have them (with both, the Poké Ball in the middle); one that misses isn't used up. While a Repel works, wild Pokémon below your Pokémon's level, or with lower base stats, stay away, and in a zone you haven't finished, every Pokémon there except its legendary is equally likely; shiny odds don't change. The backpack shows a badge with its minutes left. Only time spent actually playing counts toward a Repel: sitting in a game without touching it earns nothing.

### Moves

Your Pokémon fights with two moves, one on each battle button:

- **Right:** the strongest move of its own type that it knows, like Ember for Charmander.
- **Left:** its strongest move of any other type, like Scratch.

While it knows only one attack, that one gets a single wide button.

It knows every attack its species learns by leveling up in Pokémon Red and Blue, up to its current level, and it gains a level every hour of age, asleep or awake. Only levels teach moves: training and winning don't. When a stronger move takes over a button, the next wild battle opens with **Learned Flamethrower!** A new move that is weaker than the one on its button doesn't replace it.

Charmander, for example, starts with Scratch, learns Ember at level 9, Slash at 30 (in place of Scratch) and Flamethrower at 38 (in place of Ember). After evolving, it follows the new form's list, so a move can come a few levels earlier or later: Charmeleon learns Slash at 33 and Flamethrower at 42. Ditto has no moves of its own: it copies the wild Pokémon's. **[Moves](Catch151/docs/MOVES.md)** lists every Pokémon's moves, the level it learns each one and each move's power, so you can see whether it pays to wait before evolving.

### Training

Your Pokémon's ATK, DEF and SPD grow as it levels up, and training adds more on top:

- **Winning a battle** adds attack training: 3 a win, so 34 wins fill it.
- **Every 6 hours of good care** (every need at 40 or more) adds defense training. Sleep counts, and a need can dip under 40 for up to 15 minutes before the 6 hours start over.
- **Mini games** (Berry Drop, Rock Hopper, PikaSurf, Dungeon Crawler, Reel Fishing, Pinball) add speed training.
- **Weight** isn't training: food adds it (candy the most), and games, battles and time burn it off. Over 50, your Pokémon tires twice as fast.

Each kind of training stops at 100. The card's **Training** page (**Stats** in the ring, then two pages on) shows how far along each one is, with **MAX** when it's full, and your Pokémon's weight. Once attack training is maxed, a win says so instead of adding more. A new Pokémon starts training from zero.

### Travel

Tap the **map** icon, second from the left, to open **Travel**: **Knomi Island**, a round island with five zones round a patch of tall grass. Tap a zone (Forest, Beach, Cave, Mansion or Field) to see the Pokémon that live there, what you've caught and what's still a silhouette, and **Search** to hunt one. Common Pokémon turn up all the time, rare ones much less often, and each zone's legendary can appear on any Search, but only about once in a hundred. The tall grass in the middle, **Wild Encounter**, meets a wild Pokémon from anywhere at your Pokémon's home, just like pressing the physical button, and brings you back to the island when it's over. The arrows at the sides of a zone's list, or a swipe, turn its pages. Battles train attack. The arrow on the right of the island slides Travel out along the pier to **Knomi Tower**.

### Knomi Tower

Tap the tower on Travel's second page, then **CLIMB!** with a full energy bar. Your Pokémon goes with two friends you pick from the Pokédex, at its level and training, up eight floors (Rock, Water, Electric, Grass, Poison, Psychic, Fire and Ground) and the Legend's two fights at the top, the second against Mewtwo. HP carries over, everyone standing gets 20% back between floors, and a Pokémon that faints sleeps for the rest of the climb. **Who goes first?** marks each of your team **▲ Strong** or **▼ Weak** against the next guardian. **RUN** always works and just ends the climb. Every fight fills the balls' bars; beating the Legend earns a Master Ball every time, and the first time the Tower Master badge and the Hall of Fame. A lost climb gives the next one +5% HP (up to +15%), and once it's beaten, three challenges wait. [Everything about the tower](Catch151/docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md#knomi-tower).

### Games

Tap the **handheld** icon, on the left, for the mini games: cartridges on a Game Boy's screen, each label a little scene from its game. When one starts, a card drops in over it with how to play; press **Ready, GO!** or swipe the card away to start. They train speed, and the time you play counts toward your next Repel. At the end, **Play Again!** starts it again, the ✕ goes back to the games, and the Repel bar opens the Repel's page. **Swipe up from the lower half of the screen to quit any game**, with nothing earned.

- **Dungeon Crawler:** explore a cave floor by floor. Tap toward where you want to step, or hold to keep walking. Walk into a wild Pokémon to attack it with your own best move, pick up Razz, Oran and Pinap Berries to heal, and find the stairs down. A better-trained Pokémon gets deeper.
- **Rock Hopper:** tap to jump over rocks, bushes, fallen logs, stumps and mushrooms in a forest that keeps getting faster. How long can you last?
- **PikaSurf:** your Pokémon surfs down the rushing water on a board; tilt, drag it or use the arrow keys to dodge the rocks coming up in three lanes. Pikachu surfs alongside and never hits one, until he pulls ahead out of sight. It keeps speeding up, and one rock ends the run.
- **Reel Fishing:** five casts off the pier at the Beach, the rod in your hands. Tap the water by a fish (the bigger the shadow, the heavier it is; bubbles over one mean it's about to bite), wait out the nibbles, and tap when the float goes under. Then tap to the beat to reel it in, keeping the white needle in the green of the **LINE** bar on the right: too fast and the line snaps, too slow and it slips off, and each weight has its own pace (the fish swells on it, and on a computer the fight song plays it). Every fish is weighed, a gold gauge on the left filling to its weight and saying how big it is for its kind, then let go: the score is the total weight, against bronze, silver and gold lines at 110, 331 and 661 lb. One giant comes in every game. The Knomi's button (Space on a computer) casts, strikes and reels too.
- **Pinball:** a round table, its rim the wall. Hold the left or right half of the screen to raise that flipper; the Knomi's button (Space on a computer) raises both, and on a computer the left and right arrows raise each. Three balls. The ramp up the left comes back to your left flipper (+250), the orbit round the top lights berries (all six double your points, the next six triple them), the three bumpers bring out Diglett (+1000; beat it twice and the next is Dugtrio, three heads to hit), and the tall grass by the right wall (at night, a cave) sends a wild Pokémon into the ring in the middle: hit it with the ball, each hit your Pokémon's move, to knock it out in 20 seconds (+500).
- **Who's That Pokémon?:** name the silhouette, ten rounds per game, never the same Pokémon twice. Each one has ten seconds on the clock: run out and it counts as wrong, and the answer shows.
- **Berry Drop:** in the woods under two tall berry trees, tilt left and right, or drag your Pokémon with a finger, to catch falling berries. They fall faster with every catch, with no top speed, so every game ends.

### Medals

**Medals** in the ring opens the trophy room: all 26 medals as things in one room, the zone badges and Knomi Tower's in a case, the Pokédex statues, the shiny gem, the level 100 cups and the care rosettes. Every medal is earned once and kept through every Pokémon you raise. Every few seconds a gleam sweeps over the ones you've won; one you haven't won yet is its shape in shadow. Tap anywhere for each medal's card: how to earn it, and how far along you are where the game counts it. A new medal drops in on a ribbon; tap it to see its card.

### Buddies

If another Catch 151 joins the same Wi-Fi network, its Pokémon comes over and walks beside yours, and a round Party Boost button (two balloons and **+2**) appears in the sky to the right of yours. Up to 4 buddies can visit at once. Whenever buddies or a daily visitor are around, yours stands in the middle and a size bigger than the rest, so you can always tell which one is yours, even among Pokémon of the same species; the others stand at the sides. Tap a buddy's Pokémon to open the buddy menu for it, and swipe left or right there to switch buddies. Having a buddy earns the **First buddy** medal, and a full party of 5 Pokémon on screen earns **Party of 5**.

- **Battle:** a friendly match where each of you picks your own moves. It makes your Pokémon happy, with no training gained and no energy lost.
- **Trade:** each of you picks a Pokémon from your Pokédex, and a green dot marks ones your buddy doesn't have yet. Once you both confirm, you swap: the one you gave leaves your Pokédex. A shiny is its own square, so you can trade it and keep the regular one. Your own partner can't be traded, so with only one Pokémon, Trade is greyed out. Changed your mind? The **✕** backs out at any point before the swap, and nothing changes hands.
- **Play:** games only buddies can play together, with joy and bond for both afterwards, a bit more for the winner, and the **Playmate** medal. Each opens under its how-to-play card, and starts once you've both pressed **Ready, GO!**
  - **Who's That? Showdown** shows you both the same silhouette at the same moment: the first to tap the right name gets the point, and a wrong tap is out for that round. 10 questions.
  - **Volley Ball:** your screen is your half of a volleyball court, with the net along the top, and your Pokémon is the paddle; tilt or drag to move, and it runs left and right with you, facing you when it stands still. A ball over the top of your screen drops in on your buddy's. Every shot curves and the ball slowly speeds up, so a match to 3 points lasts about a minute.
  - **PikaSurf:** you both surf the same course at once, your buddy's Pokémon riding alongside yours. Whoever lasts longer wins; the first to crash waits for the other to finish, and then you're both back on Play together.

### Party Boost

Your Pokémon does better with company. While at least one buddy or the daily visitor is around:

- **Energy drains a quarter slower.**
- **+2 ATK, DEF and SPD** in wild battles and Dungeon Crawler.

It's the same boost however many friends are around. Tap the Party Boost button to see who's here and what you get. Tap a friend there to see its stats: level, HP, ATK, DEF and SPD, and a buddy's Pokédex count. Swipe from there to a buddy's Battle, Trade and Play menu, and the ✕ goes back to Party Boost. Tapping a buddy's Pokémon on the main screen still opens its menu first, with its stats a swipe away. While the boost is on, the Battle Stats page shows each stat with its **+2**. Battles between buddies don't use it, since both sides would have it.

To find each other, Catch 151 games share their Pokémon's species, level and nickname and their Pokédex with other devices on the same network. Buddies work between Knomis, the Mac and Windows apps and the browser version, in any mix.

### Daily visitor

Once a day, a wild Pokémon drops by with a candy, a third one for that day, and stays for an hour. It joins any buddies in the row, counts for the Party Boost, and shows how many minutes it has left on the Party Boost screen. It only arrives while yours is awake, but once it's here it stays its hour, even if yours goes to sleep. It remembers its time even if the Knomi is unplugged. When its hour is up, it says goodbye and cheers yours up.

### Wi-Fi and the clock

The Knomi has no clock battery, so it gets the time over Wi-Fi. If it was already on your network, it usually reconnects by itself.

To change networks, open **Settings** in the ring, turn to the next page, tap **Change Wi-Fi**, pick your network, and type the password on the screen. A new network is only saved once it connects.

On Wi-Fi the clock sets itself, and swiping down just shows it (**Set by Wi-Fi**). To choose your time zone, tap the city under **Time zone** and use the arrows: each city shows its own time, so pick the one that matches your clock, then tap **OK**. Summer time changes by itself. A new Knomi starts on New York.

Without Wi-Fi you set the time by hand on the same screen. The Knomi can't keep time while it's unplugged, so set it again after that: a time earlier than the one it shows means the next morning.

Changing the clock doesn't speed anything up. Time away only counts by network time, and a new day (candies, the visitor, the streak) needs 12 hours of play since the last one began. [How it works](Catch151/docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md#time) has the details.

### Updates over Wi-Fi

Once it's on Wi-Fi, the Knomi looks for a new version two minutes after it starts and then every hour, while the screen is dimmed. With **Auto-update** on (the switch on the second page of **Settings** in the ring; it's on unless you turn it off), it installs the new version by itself the next time nobody is using it: the screen dimmed on any page but a game, a battle or a question, between 7 am and 8 pm (10 am to 6 pm while your Pokémon sleeps). Knomis on 2.1.0 or older only install by themselves from the home screen with the Pokémon awake, so 2.1.1 may need one update by hand. To update by hand, open **Settings** in the ring, turn to the next page and tap **Check for update**. If a new version is out, the button changes to **Update to v…**: tap it and wait about a minute while it downloads and restarts. Your Pokémon, Pokédex and Wi-Fi are kept. If the new version ever fails to start, the Knomi goes back to the one it had.

The version you have is on **About**, the last page of **Settings**, under the 3D Print SOS coin (tap it and it spins):

<p align="center"><img src="Catch151/docs/images/v2/about.gif" alt="The About page: the 3D Print SOS coin spins twice and lands with a gold gleam, over the Catch 151 logo, v2.2.5, and the GitHub and YouTube buttons" width="240"></p>

On version 1.30 or older, **Update** could fail while buddies were connected through the internet relay: the two secure connections didn't fit in memory together. If yours says "Update failed", install 1.31 or newer once with the [web installer](https://fedorsosnin.github.io/Catch151/) and a USB cable, without ticking "Erase device". It keeps your Pokémon, and from then on updates over Wi-Fi work.

## Going back to the stock firmware

BIGTREETECH publishes the original firmware and instructions in its [KNOMI repository](https://github.com/bigtreetech/KNOMI). Installing it removes the game.

If you'd like an exact copy of your Knomi before you start, back it up first. The file includes any saved Wi-Fi password, so keep it private:

```bash
esptool --port YOUR_PORT read-flash 0 ALL knomi-backup.bin
```

## Build it yourself

The project builds with [arduino-cli](https://arduino.github.io/arduino-cli/) and ESP32 core 3.3.10. Everything is in the [`Catch151`](Catch151) folder, which is also the Arduino sketch (`Catch151.ino`): every command below runs from inside it.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/FedorSosnin/Catch151.git
```

```bash
cd Catch151/Catch151
```

```bash
arduino-cli config init --additional-urls https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/gh-pages/package_esp32_index.json
```

```bash
arduino-cli core update-index
```

```bash
arduino-cli core install esp32:esp32@3.3.10
```

```bash
arduino-cli lib install "GFX Library for Arduino" SensorLib XPowersLib U8g2
```

```bash
bash tools/build_knomi_web.sh
```

The build needs Python 3 and writes the flashable files to `web/knomi/firmware`. On a Mac, `tools/knomi_flash.sh` builds and flashes in one step.

Game rules run on your computer without a board:

```bash
./test/run_tests.sh
```

The code also builds for the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75, a bigger round board with a microSD slot. [`docs/WAVESHARE.md`](Catch151/docs/WAVESHARE.md) explains how to build it, flash it and load the sprites.

## Catch 151 for Web

**[Play in your browser](https://catch151.3dprintsos.com)** on a computer, phone or tablet: the same game in a Poké Ball ring on a black page, with the CRT screen. Nothing to install. On a phone, **Add to Home Screen** makes it a full-screen app.

- **Saves:** it saves in the browser by itself. Clearing the browser's data erases it, so use **Export save** now and then for a backup file, and **Import save** to bring a Pokémon back or move it to another browser. Coming from V1, your Pokémon comes along by itself, and V1's save stays as it was.
- **One tab at a time:** a second tab offers **Play here**, which moves the game over after the first one saves.
- **Controls:** tap, hold and swipe the screen, as on the Knomi (see [How to play](#how-to-play)). On a phone, tilt it to aim the ball and to steer in PikaSurf and Berry Drop; where the browser asks first, **Enable tilt** turns it on. On Android it also buzzes along with the sounds; **Vibration** turns that off (iPhone browsers can't vibrate). On a computer, the mouse is a finger that presses when you click, the left and right arrows tilt, all four arrows walk in Dungeon Crawler, Space is the Knomi's button (in Reel Fishing it casts, strikes and reels; in Pinball the left and right arrows raise the flippers and Space both), and on a catch the left and right arrows choose a ball and Space or the up arrow throws it.
- **Size and CRT:** it opens Large, with the CRT screen on. **Size** makes it Small or Medium, like the Mac app, and on a small screen it shrinks to fit. **CRT** turns the effect off for the Knomi's own look; a browser without WebGL 2 shows that look by itself.
- **Sound and Music:** **Sound** turns the effects and cries off and on, and **Music** steps the music down from High to Medium, Low and Off, each on its own. Settings in the ring has a level from 0 to 3 for each.
- **Buddies:** browsers can't send the Knomi's local Wi-Fi messages, so they meet buddies through a small relay on the internet that only passes those same messages between devices on the same home network and stores nothing. Knomis and the Mac and Windows apps use it too, so they all meet each other.
- **Updates:** it updates itself: reload to get the newest version. A tab left open checks every hour and says when there is one; with **Auto-update** on (the button under the game; on unless you turn it off), it reloads onto it by itself once nobody has touched it for a minute on the home screen.

To build it yourself, run `webgame/build.sh` (needs [Emscripten](https://emscripten.org)); `tools/publish_web.sh` publishes it. The relay's code is in [relay/](Catch151/relay/).

## Catch 151 for Mac

The same game as a round window on your Mac desktop, in a Poké Ball ring with a soft shadow under it. It has no Dock icon; it lives in the menu bar. It needs an Apple silicon Mac (M1 or later) with macOS 12 Monterey or newer.

1. Download `Catch151.dmg` from the [latest release](https://github.com/FedorSosnin/Catch151/releases), open it and drag **Catch 151** to **Applications**. (Had PokeMacGochi? It updates itself to Catch 151 and keeps your pet.)
2. Open it once. macOS warns that it can't check the app, because it isn't signed by an Apple developer account. Click **Done**.
3. Go to **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down and click **Open Anyway** next to Catch 151. After that it opens normally.

   Or, in Terminal: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Catch 151.app"`

**Controls:** click, hold and swipe like a touch screen. The left and right arrows tilt, for Berry Drop and throwing Poké Balls, and all four arrows walk in Dungeon Crawler. Space is the Knomi's button, for a wild battle (in Reel Fishing it casts, strikes and reels; in Pinball the left and right arrows raise the flippers and Space both); on a catch, the left and right arrows choose a ball and Space or the up arrow throws it. Drag the red and white ring to move the window: it grows when the mouse is over it, so it's easy to grab, and the screen inside is only for touching, with the mouse as a finger. Right-click it, or click the Poké Ball in the menu bar, for About, Size, CRT Screen, Mute Sound, Music (Off, Low, Medium or High), Check for Updates, Update Automatically and Quit.

**Size and CRT Screen:** it opens Large, with the CRT screen on. **Size** makes it Medium or Small, sharp at every size, and **CRT Screen** turns the effect off for the Knomi's own look.

Your pet, the sound, music and CRT settings, and the window's size and place are saved on the Mac in `~/Library/Application Support/PokeMacGochi` (the folder keeps the game's old name), separate from any Knomi. Only one copy runs on that save at a time: opening it again just tells you it's already open.

**Updates:** Catch 151 checks for a new version 10 seconds after it opens and then every hour, counting the time the Mac sleeps, so a version out overnight is there by morning. With **Update Automatically** checked in its menu (on unless you turn it off), it installs the new version by itself once nobody has touched it for a minute on the home screen, and reopens on it. Turned off, it tells you once when one is out. From 1.30 on it updates itself, like the Knomi: click **Update** (or **Update to Version …** in its menu) and it downloads the new version, checks its signature, and reopens on it a few seconds later. Your pet stays, since it's saved outside the app. Older versions get the new DMG instead: quit the app, open the DMG and drag it into Applications, choosing **Replace**.

**Buddies:** a Knomi or another Mac on the same Wi-Fi shows up as a buddy, just like between two Knomis. The first time, macOS asks whether Catch 151 may find devices on your local network: click **Allow**. If you clicked **Don't Allow**, turn Catch 151 on in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network** and reopen it.

To build it yourself, run `mac/build_dmg.sh` (needs Xcode's Command Line Tools). [host/README.md](Catch151/host/README.md) explains how it works.

## Catch 151 for Windows

The same game as a round window on your Windows desktop, in a Poké Ball ring with a soft shadow under it, for Windows 10 and 11. It's one file with nothing to install, it has no taskbar button, and it lives in the taskbar's notification area (the icons by the clock).

1. Download `Catch151.exe` from the [latest Windows release](https://github.com/FedorSosnin/Catch151/releases) and put it somewhere you'll keep it, like your Desktop or Documents.
2. Open it. Windows warns that it **protected your PC**, because the app isn't signed by a paid developer certificate. Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. After that it opens normally.
3. The first time, Windows Firewall may ask whether Catch 151 can use your network. Allow it on **private networks**, so buddies on your Wi-Fi can find you.

**Controls:** the same as the Mac app. Click, hold and swipe like a touch screen; the left and right arrows tilt, all four arrows walk in Dungeon Crawler, and Space is the Knomi's button; on a catch, the left and right arrows choose a ball and Space or the up arrow throws it. Drag the ring to move the window: it grows when the mouse is over it, and the screen inside is only for touching. Right-click it, or click the Poké Ball by the clock, for About, Size, CRT Screen, Mute Sound, Music (Off, Low, Medium or High), Check for Updates, Update Automatically and Quit. To start it with Windows, put a shortcut to it in the Startup folder (press Win+R and type `shell:startup`).

It opens Large, with the CRT screen, like the Mac app: **Size** and **CRT Screen** in its menu change them.

Your pet, the sound, music and CRT settings, and the window's size and place are saved in `%APPDATA%\PokeWinGochi` (the folder keeps the game's old name), separate from the app; the game's pictures are unpacked to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\PokeWinGochi`. Only one copy runs on that save at a time.

**Updates:** it checks for a new version when it opens and then every hour, counting the time the PC sleeps. With **Update Automatically** checked in its menu (on unless you turn it off), it installs the new version by itself once nobody has touched it for a minute on the home screen, and starts again on it. Turned off, it tells you once when one is out. From 1.13 on it updates itself, like the Knomi: click **Yes** (or **Update to Version …** in its menu) and it downloads the new version, checks its signature, and starts again on it a few seconds later. Your pet stays. On older versions, quit the app, download the new `Catch151.exe` and put it in place of the old one.

**Tilt and controllers:** a controller's left stick tilts smoothly (for Berry Drop and aiming Poké Balls), and the stick or D-pad walks in Dungeon Crawler: the built-in one of a handheld PC like the ROG Ally, or an Xbox controller. If Windows has a motion sensor, as many tablets do, tilting the device tilts the game, like on a phone; **Tilt with the Device** in the menu turns that off. On a ROG Ally, set the gyro to act as the left joystick in Armoury Crate, and tilting the Ally tilts the game. The controller and the sensor only count while PokeWinGochi is the active window.

**Vibration:** on a handheld PC with a built-in controller, like the ROG Ally, or with an Xbox controller connected, it buzzes softly with taps, meals, medals and catches. **Vibration** in the menu turns it off; it only shows when a controller is connected.

**Buddies:** Knomis, Macs, PCs and browsers find each other, the same as the Mac app.

To build it yourself, run `windows/build.sh` on a Mac or Linux with [llvm-mingw](https://github.com/mstorsjo/llvm-mingw). [host/README.md](Catch151/host/README.md) explains how it works.
